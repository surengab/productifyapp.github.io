Five habit tracker examples you can adapt

A useful entry names an action, a schedule, and a finish line. These are example targets, not requirements. Make them smaller or change the timing to fit your day.

Turn a broad intention into something you can record
IdeaExample scheduleWhat to log
Read moreAfter dinner, daily10 minutes read
Move regularlyAfter lunch on weekdaysA walk of a length that works for you
Practice a skillMonday, Wednesday, Friday15 minutes of practice
Prepare for tomorrowBefore finishing workWrite tomorrow's first task
Review the weekSunday afternoonWrite one thing to keep and one to change

For something you only need to finish once, use a to-do list. For an action you want to repeat, use a habit tracker. See how the two differ.

How to choose the right habits to track

Before picking from the list below, apply three filters to any habit you're considering:

  • Is it specific? "Exercise" is not trackable. "Walk for 30 minutes" is. You need to be able to answer "did I do this?" with a clear yes or no.
  • Is it daily (or on a fixed schedule)? Habit trackers work best for behaviours you intend to do every day, or on set days of the week. Use a schedule that reflects when you realistically intend to do the action.
  • Do you actually want this? Choose an action with a reason that matters to you. A smaller habit you want to practice is a useful place to start.

Start with one habit if the list feels overwhelming. You can keep the other ideas for later. Add another when the first fits your routine and checking in is manageable.

💪

Health & fitness habits

These are common areas people choose to track. Select actions and targets that fit your circumstances and any relevant professional advice.

  • Daily movement (20-30 min) Walk, run, cycle, swim, any intentional movement. Choose a form of movement that fits your abilities and preferences.
  • Keep water available Refill your water bottle at a regular point in your day. Choose an amount appropriate to your needs.
  • Consistent sleep time Track whether you went to bed at your target time. Use the record to notice how your schedule changes across the week.
  • 10 minutes of stretching Choose movements that are comfortable for you and a time when you can practice them.
  • No alcohol on weekdays A clear, binary target. Choose a target that fits your circumstances.
  • Eat vegetables with every meal Simpler than tracking calories; creates a positive default rather than a restriction.
🧠

Mind & focus habits

These habits protect and develop your capacity for deep work, creative thinking, and continuous learning, the compounding assets of a knowledge worker's life.

  • Read 10-20 pages Non-fiction or fiction, consistent reading compounds into extraordinary breadth of knowledge over years.
  • 90-minute deep work session One uninterrupted focus block per day. Phone away, notifications off. Track whether it happened.
  • No social media before 10am Protects your morning from reactive, distracted thinking. One of the highest-leverage digital hygiene habits.
  • Learn something new (15 min) Language app, online course, podcast on a new subject, any intentional learning beyond your current domain.
  • Review tomorrow's priorities tonight Write down the first task you want to work on tomorrow, then check it when your day begins.
  • No phone 1 hour before sleep Try putting your phone away during your wind-down routine and record whether the plan fits.
🌅

Morning routine habits

Choose a morning habit that fits between things you already do, such as waking, breakfast, and leaving home. For a full guide, see our morning routine page.

  • Wake up at target time Choose a realistic wake time and record how often your schedule allows it.
  • Drink water immediately on waking You've been fasting for 7-8 hours. Hydration first thing is an easy, immediate win.
  • 5 minutes of journaling Free writing, gratitude log, or intention-setting. Clarifies thinking and sets a calm, intentional tone.
  • Morning sunlight (10 min outside) Spend a little time outside if it suits your schedule and circumstances.
  • Prepare breakfast Choose a simple breakfast and prepare what you need ahead of time.
  • Prepare your bag for the day Check for the items you will need before you leave home.
🧘

Mental wellbeing habits

These are ideas for making space to pause and reflect. Choose an activity you find useful and adapt its length to your day.

  • 5-10 minutes of meditation Any method: guided, breath-focused, or body scan. Start with a length of time that feels manageable.
  • Gratitude (3 things) Write down three specific things you're grateful for. Use a notebook or a note on your phone.
  • Spend time in nature (20 min) Choose an outdoor setting you enjoy, such as a park or garden.
  • No news consumption before noon Choose a later time to check the news and record whether you kept that boundary.
  • Breathwork (5 min) Set aside a few quiet minutes to pause and notice your breathing if you find that comfortable.
  • End-of-day shutdown ritual Close your work tabs and write down tomorrow's first task. Record whether you finished this short routine.
🤝

Relationships & growth habits

Connection, creativity, and learning may also deserve a place in your routine. Choose what matters to you instead of limiting the list to work and physical activity.

  • Reach out to one person Text, call, or message one friend, family member, or colleague. Maintains social connections without requiring scheduled time.
  • Write 200 words (any topic) Daily writing practice builds communication skills and creative thinking across all domains, not just writing itself.
  • Practice a skill for 20 minutes Instrument, language, sport, coding, drawing, deliberate practice on a skill you want to develop.
  • No complaints for the day A challenging but powerful habit. Track it as a binary yes/no. Shifts your attentional default from problems to solutions.
  • One act of generosity Any form: compliment, help offered, favour done. Choose an action the other person is likely to welcome.
  • Weekly review (on Sundays) Review the week: what worked, what didn't, what to adjust. Track as a weekly habit. The most underrated productivity habit.

Set up one habit, then review the first week

Suppose you choose reading. In Productify, create a reading habit, set a target such as 10 minutes, choose the days you intend to read, and add a reminder after dinner. You can create a custom habit or start from the template library.

  1. Choose the smallest useful version. If 10 minutes is too much for a busy evening, start with a shorter reading session.
  2. Record what happened. Log the time you actually read. For a check-off habit, mark it complete only when the action is done.
  3. Review the scheduled days. Notice which days you missed and whether the timing or target got in the way.
  4. Change one thing. Try an earlier reminder, a smaller goal, or a different schedule for the next week.

A week of check-ins shows how the setup fits your life; it does not prove a habit is automatic. Read what habit-formation timelines mean before treating a streak as a deadline.
