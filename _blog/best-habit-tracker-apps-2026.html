What does a free habit tracker app include?

Separate a free download from a free routine you can keep using. Productify includes up to 5 habits, reminders, history, and 5 starter templates. Habit Duo and AI insights are Pro features. Habitica offers a free core experience with optional purchases, while Streaks has an upfront price.

Before choosing, check whether the free version covers your intended number of habits, reminders, devices, and history. A paid feature is only useful if it solves a problem in your routine. Review Productify's free and paid limits and the official listings below.

The six habit tracker apps, with tradeoffs

Productify: for iPhone habits and a personal routine

Productify combines habit logging with measurable goals, reminders, templates, and progress views. Its Apple Watch companion lets you record activity from your wrist. Habit Duo adds a way to work on a shared commitment with another person.

Why shortlist it: you want to organize a small set of habits on iPhone and connect daily actions with goals. Tradeoff: choose around the platforms you actually need; an iPhone-centered workflow may not fit someone who relies on Android or a browser.

Streaks: for Apple devices and Health-linked tracking

Streaks lists support for iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Vision, and Apple Watch, with iCloud synchronization. It supports up to 24 tasks, timed and negative tasks, notes, task sharing, and automatic completion for compatible Health-linked activities.

Why shortlist it: you already use Apple devices and want some activities recorded through Health. Tradeoff: the US store lists an upfront price of $5.99. Check that the particular activity you want can be tracked automatically rather than assuming every task will work that way.

HabitNow: for Android habits and task planning

HabitNow combines habits and to-dos with daily, weekly, and monthly schedules. Its Android listing describes timers, reminders, notes, widgets, and progress charts. This makes it worth considering when recurring routines and one-off tasks need to sit close together.

Why shortlist it: you use Android and need more than a daily yes-or-no checklist. Tradeoff: the listing includes in-app purchases, so verify the limits of the free version against your actual plan. A free download does not mean every feature is included.

Habitica: for game rewards and shared challenges

Habitica turns completed habits and tasks into rewards for a game character. It includes recurring tasks, to-dos, parties, and shared challenges. The developer describes the core experience as free, with optional purchases and subscriptions.

Why shortlist it: character progression and a group give you a reason to return. Tradeoff: those same mechanics add things to manage. If you mainly want a quiet record, try the daily interaction before committing to the game around it.

How to choose without downloading everything

  1. Write one real habit: include its schedule and what counts as done.
  2. Choose your device: eliminate options that do not fit where you need to log it.
  3. Pick one essential feature: a timer, Health integration, reminders, or a partner, for example.
  4. Try a normal week where possible: include a busy day. Notice whether the check-in fits your routine.
  5. Review the cost: distinguish a free download, a free plan, a trial, and an ongoing subscription.

A one-week trial is a usability check, not a test of whether a habit has formed. You can judge whether an app is awkward before the behavior feels automatic. See what habit-formation timelines actually mean.

What to check in any habit tracker

Can you find today’s action, log it accurately, and correct a mistake? Does the schedule match your life? What happens to the display after a missed day? Can you understand a week of results? These questions are more useful than an unsupported claim that one extra tap determines how long you will keep a habit.

For a practical comparison, use the same proposed habit in the apps you shortlist. Record your own observations about setup, reminders, and review. Those notes describe your experience; they do not establish that one app produces better outcomes for everyone.

What a habit tracker records

A tracker records repeated actions against a schedule. That can help you see consistency and spot obstacles, but a checked box is a record of behavior, not proof of motivation or automaticity. Start with the beginner’s setup guide if tracking itself is new to you.

Habit tracker or to-do list?

Use a to-do list to organize tasks you need to finish; use a habit tracker to review a behavior across repeated opportunities. Some apps combine both. A paper notebook can also do the job if you are comfortable making your own grid and reviewing it. Our tracker versus to-do list guide explains the distinction.
