## What habit streaks are

A habit streak is a count of consecutive days you've completed a habit without missing. The streak number grows each day you check in and resets when you miss a day.

In Productify, streaks are displayed prominently for each habit — giving you an at-a-glance view of your current momentum.

---

## The psychology of streaks

Streaks work through several psychological mechanisms:

**Commitment and consistency** (Cialdini): Once we've started something, we feel compelled to continue. A 14-day streak is a commitment that makes skipping feel costly.

**Loss aversion** (Kahneman): The pain of losing something is approximately twice as powerful as the pleasure of gaining an equivalent amount. The prospect of losing a streak motivates action on low-energy days more effectively than the prospect of extending it.

**Identity accumulation**: Every streak day is evidence of the identity you're building. "I've exercised 23 days in a row" is not just data — it's proof that you are the kind of person who exercises.

**Visual feedback**: Seeing a growing chain of completed days makes abstract progress concrete and visible, which is independently motivating.

---

## Streaks without the anxiety

The most common criticism of streak-based apps is that they create all-or-nothing pressure. Missing one day resets everything, and that loss can be so discouraging that users abandon the habit entirely.

Productify's approach:
- **Streaks are displayed but not weaponised** — there are no alarm notifications warning you that your streak is "at risk"
- **Your full completion history is always visible** — a missed day is one mark in a sea of completed ones, not an erasure of everything
- **The focus is on your personal best and overall rate**, not just current streak

---

## Using streaks effectively

- **Track your personal best** — it becomes a natural target
- **Apply the "never miss twice" rule** — one miss is noise, two in a row is a pattern
- **Use streaks as feedback, not as judgement** — a low streak week shows where to redesign, not where you failed
