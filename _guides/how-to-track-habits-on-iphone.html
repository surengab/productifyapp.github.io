You don’t need to download anything to start tracking a habit on your iPhone. Reminders, the Health app and Journal each cover part of the job. None of them was designed as a habit tracker, though, so each leaves something out. Choose by the habit, not by the app.

1. Reminders: the quickest way to track any habit

Reminders works for almost any habit, because a repeating reminder is just an action, a time and a schedule.

  1. Create a list called Habits in the Reminders app, so habits don’t mix with errands.
  2. Add one reminder per habit, written as an action with a minimum: “Read 10 pages” rather than “Read more.”
  3. Make it repeat. Tap the info button, turn on Date and Time, tap Repeat, and choose Daily, Weekdays or Custom to pick specific days. In iOS 27, Repeat also appears in the options box under a reminder while you type.
  4. Tick the circle when it’s done. The reminder moves on to the next scheduled day.
  5. Add the Reminders widget to your Home Screen and point it at the Habits list, so you see what’s left without opening the app.

Where it falls short: Reminders shows what is due, not how you have been doing. Completed items stay hidden unless you choose Show Completed, there is no streak or chart, and a skipped day shows up as an overdue reminder rather than as a gap in a record. It also can’t express “three times a week, any days”; you have to pick fixed days. That gap is the core of the habit tracker vs to-do list difference.

2. Health › Medications: vitamins and supplements

The Medications feature in the Health app isn’t only for prescriptions. Apple says you can add vitamins and supplements the same way, which makes it a capable tracker for a habit such as “vitamin D with breakfast.”

  • Schedule: set the time of day, specific days of the week or a repeating cycle, with optional start and end dates. Specific days and cycles need iOS 18 or later.
  • Reminders: turn on Dose Reminders. If you haven’t logged a dose, a follow-up alert arrives 30 minutes later.
  • Logging: go to Health › Browse › Medications, tap the plus button next to an item, and choose Taken or Skipped.
  • History: tap an item to see how often you took or skipped it.

Where it falls short: it only makes sense for things you take. For prescribed medicines, treat the log as a record, not a substitute for your doctor’s or pharmacist’s instructions.

3. Health › State of Mind: a daily mood check-in

If the habit is noticing how you feel, the Health app has a mood log built in (iOS 17 or later). Go to Health › Browse › Mental Wellbeing and set up State of Mind logging. Each entry is either a momentary emotion or your mood for the whole day, on a slider from unpleasant to pleasant, with optional labels for what is affecting you, such as work or fitness. Reminders default to midday and evening, and you can change the times. Past entries are under Summary › State of Mind, with Show in Charts for the bigger picture.

Steps work without any logging at all: Health counts them from your iPhone’s motion data. If your habit is walking more, “check today’s steps in the evening” can be the whole system.

Where it falls short: you get charts of mood and steps, not a tick for a habit, and nothing links a good day to the habit behind it unless you add a note.

4. Journal: a streak for a writing habit

Apple’s Journal app (iOS 17.2 or later) tracks a streak for journaling itself. Its Insights show your current streak, the number of entries, words written and a calendar of the days you wrote, and it can send a notification to help you keep a streak going. In iOS 27 you can set the streak to daily, weekly or automatic, which matters if you are aiming for three entries a week rather than one a day.

After an entry, you can log how you feel, and Journal saves it to Health as well. Time spent journaling can also count as mindful minutes in Health.

Where it falls short: it tracks one habit, writing.

5. A habit tracker app: every habit in one place

The built-in options spread your habits across three apps, and only Journal shows a streak. A habit tracker earns its place when you want:

  • one list with every habit you are building, checked off with a tap;
  • a streak and a full history for each habit, so a good month stays visible after a missed day;
  • a weekly target, such as “strength training three times a week, any days”;
  • an amount instead of a tick, such as 20 minutes or 8 glasses of water;
  • reminders and a Home Screen widget for each habit.

Give each habit one home

Mixing options is fine, as long as each habit lives in one place. One reasonable setup: supplements in Medications, mood in State of Mind, writing in Journal, and the one to three habits you are actively building in a single tracker. Logging the same habit in two apps doubles the effort, and the duplicate is usually the first thing to lapse.

Whatever you use, review it weekly

A record helps most when you look back at it. Across 138 studies, a 2016 meta-analysis by Harkin and colleagues found that prompting people to monitor their progress improved goal attainment on average. It doesn’t show that any particular app, or streaks in general, will work for you.

Once a week, open whatever you use and ask what got in the way on the days you missed.
