What is a year in pixels?

A year in pixels is a popular bullet-journal layout: a grid with one square for each day of the year. You choose one thing to record, give each possible value a color, and fill in one square at the end of every day. It takes a few seconds, and after a month or two the colors begin to show patterns, such as better days after exercise or harder weeks at the end of a busy month.

For a habit, a year in pixels works best alongside a daily checklist. The 30-day habit tracker helps you start the habit; the year in pixels shows how it held up over months. More ideas are in what habits to track.

How to choose your colors

  • Use shades of one color. Light to dark is easier to read at a glance than five unrelated colors, and it works better for people with color vision deficiency.
  • Keep the darkest color for the end you care about most, for example your best days, so good stretches stand out.
  • Test your pens first. Colored pencils and fine markers work well. Try them in the key squares, and print on thicker paper if markers bleed.
  • Add patterns if you print in black and white. A dot, a line, a cross, half-filled and filled are five clear values with a single pen.

How to keep filling it in

Link the sheet to something you already do every evening, such as brushing your teeth or plugging in your phone, and keep a pen next to it. If you miss a few days, fill them in from memory or leave them blank and carry on. A few gaps do not spoil the picture.

Once a month, look at the last few columns and ask what the colors have in common. That review is where a year in pixels becomes useful rather than just pretty. If you notice that one habit lines up with better days, the daily habit guide shows how to make it a regular part of your week.
